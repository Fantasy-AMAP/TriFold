TriFold encodes variable-cardinality 3D Gaussian scenes into fixed-layout triplane latents that jointly represent geometry and appearance, and decodes content-adaptive Gaussian sets with variable local densities.

Recent progress in 3D generation has motivated growing interest in compact generative representations of complex 3D scenes. For native 3D Gaussian scenes, however, learning such representations remains challenging due to widely varying primitive counts, nonuniform spatial density, and differences in geometry and appearance even among spatially neighboring primitives. In this work, we introduce TriFold, a native 3D Gaussian VAE that encodes Gaussian scenes into a fixed-layout triplane latent and jointly reconstructs their geometry and appearance. The key insight is to impose structural regularity on the latent space while preserving the spatial adaptivity of the underlying Gaussians. During encoding, we use cell-wise learned queries to aggregate projected Gaussians into multiple slots per cell, reducing the information loss caused by single-feature pooling. During decoding, occupancy prediction identifies active regions, where query-based set prediction and candidate selection reconstruct local Gaussian sets with spatially varying primitive counts. Building on this representation, we train a latent flow-matching model for conditional scene-level 3DGS generation, enabling point-cloud-conditioned synthesis and single-image-conditioned amodal generation. Experiments demonstrate that TriFold achieves high-fidelity reconstruction and generation from a single image that provides only a partial observation of the scene, validating its effectiveness for native 3D Gaussian representation learning.

Overview

The triplane encoder maps a variable-cardinality Gaussian set to a fixed-layout variational latent, and the triplane decoder reconstructs spatially adaptive local Gaussian sets. The learned latent further supports image- and point-cloud-conditioned scene generation with flow matching.

TriFold is a variational autoencoder that operates directly on native 3D Gaussian primitives. The triplane encoder extracts context-aware primitive features with a point transformer (PTv3) and projects them onto three orthogonal planes. Instead of pooling all primitives that collide in a cell into a single vector, Q learned queries per cell aggregate them into multiple feature slots, and a sparse cross-plane attention module exchanges information between corresponding cells before a variational bottleneck produces the triplane latent.

The triplane decoder adopts a set-based decoding paradigm without rigid 3D discretization. An occupancy predictor first localizes active regions; for each occupied voxel, a DETR-style set decoder predicts Gaussian candidates, and entropy-regularized optimal transport matches them to the local target set. The predefined query count only bounds local capacity — both the spatial allocation and the per-voxel primitive count adapt to scene content. Gaussian centers are supervised through optimal-transport matching, while the remaining geometric and appearance attributes are constrained by a full-scene rendering loss.

On the frozen latent space, a conditional latent flow-matching model supports point-cloud-conditioned synthesis from complete spatial observations and single-image-conditioned amodal generation, inferring plausible geometry and appearance for unobserved regions.

Scene Reconstruction

We compare TriFold against Can3Tok, DiffGS, and GaussianGPT under the same scene-level training and evaluation protocol. TriFold achieves the best results across all metrics, improving PSNR over GaussianGPT by 5.15 dB while reducing LPIPS and Chamfer Distance.

Scene Generation

Built on the TriFold latent space, a latent flow-matching model supports two conditioning settings: point-cloud-conditioned synthesis from complete spatial observations, and single-image-conditioned amodal generation, where a single image provides only a partial observation of the scene.
